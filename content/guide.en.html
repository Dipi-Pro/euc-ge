An electric unicycle is a single-wheel electric vehicle that keeps its own balance. It is compact, light enough to carry, and in city traffic it is often quicker than a car. This page explains what it is, how it works, what it costs and how to choose one.

What it is called

The same vehicle goes by many names, and all of them mean the same thing:

  • electric unicycle, EUC, e-unicycle
  • monowheel, electric monowheel
  • in Georgian: მონობორბალი, მონოციკლი, მონოვილი, ცალბორბალა, ელექტრო უნიციკლი
  • in Russian: моноколесо

How it works

The motor is built into the wheel hub. The shell around it holds the battery and a controller that uses a gyroscope and an accelerometer to measure tilt hundreds of times a second, driving the motor so the wheel stays upright. The rider stands on two pedals, one on each side of the wheel:

  • lean forward to accelerate;
  • lean back to brake;
  • turn by rotating your hips and shoulders, much like skiing.

When braking, the motor feeds some energy back into the battery. Speed limits, lights and battery health are shown and set in a phone app.

Who it is for

  • Daily city trips: skip the traffic and forget about parking.
  • Together with a car: it fits in the boot, so you can park at the edge of town and ride in.
  • Fun and travel: parks, trails and long rides out of the city.

Is it hard to learn?

Anyone who puts their mind to it can learn. Most people need between a day and a week. See First steps for beginners for how to start, and Safety and protective gear for what to wear.
